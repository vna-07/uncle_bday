    Dear Malume Lukashe,

    Malume, today the whole calendar bows a little. The eleventh of September belongs to you.

    You have spent your life with your hands inside the engines of other people's lives. Cars that coughed and gave up on the side of the road came to you, and you sent them home running. That is not a small thing. That is a man who fixes what the world has written off.

    I want you to know that I saw it. I saw the early mornings and the oil that never fully washed off. I saw you carry a family, a name and a whole community on shoulders that never once asked for applause. You worked so that people like me could stand where we stand.

    I am the man I am today because of you. Every bit of discipline I have, every time I refuse to quit halfway, every time I look at a problem and think there is a way through this — that is your voice in me.

    You are the reason I could reach where I am. I will forever be grateful to be your nephew. Truly, forever.

    I hope you and Auntie are well and being good to each other, the way you always have been. May the two of you enjoy this day slowly, with good food, good music and no one calling about a broken gearbox.

    Happy birthday, Mr Lukashe. May God stretch your years, keep your hands steady, your heart light and your home full. All the best, always.

    Hands that fixed everything they touched

    "You are supposed to reach where I am, as a man." You said it — and then you built the road there with your own two hands.

    With all my love — Ayolela (Ozzie)
